EVIDENCE-BASED ANXIETY DISORDER TREATMENT GUIDELINES

COGNITIVE BEHAVIORAL THERAPY FOR ANXIETY DISORDERS

Core CBT Components:
1. Psychoeducation: Teaching the patient about the nature of anxiety and its biological function
2. Cognitive Restructuring: Identifying and challenging anxious thoughts and catastrophic predictions
3. Behavioral Experiments: Testing anxious predictions in real situations to gather evidence
4. Exposure Therapy: Graduated systematic exposure to feared stimuli and avoided situations
5. Relapse Prevention: Skills maintenance and early warning sign identification and response

EXPOSURE THERAPY PROTOCOL
Develop a fear hierarchy collaboratively with the patient from least to most feared situations.
Patient rates each situation on the SUDS scale from 0 (no anxiety) to 100 (maximum anxiety).
Begin exposure exercises at approximately 30 to 40 on the SUDS scale for initial success.
Remain in each situation until anxiety reduces by at least 50 percent through habituation.
New safety learning competes with and gradually overcomes the original fear memory.
Never end an exposure exercise while anxiety is still escalating.

MINDFULNESS-BASED STRESS REDUCTION (MBSR)
8-week structured group program developed by Jon Kabat-Zinn.
Core practices: body scan meditation, sitting meditation, mindful movement and yoga.
Evidence shows MBSR reduces GAD-7 scores by an average of 3 to 4 points.
Particularly effective for chronic worry and generalized anxiety presentations.

MINDFULNESS-BASED COGNITIVE THERAPY (MBCT)
Integrates mindfulness meditation practices with cognitive therapy techniques.
Particularly effective for anxiety that occurs alongside comorbid depression.
Significantly reduces relapse rates for both anxiety and depression when practiced regularly.

ACCEPTANCE AND COMMITMENT THERAPY (ACT)
Core therapeutic goal is psychological flexibility rather than anxiety elimination.
Teaches acceptance of anxious thoughts and feelings rather than fighting against them.
Values-based committed action despite the presence of anxiety.
Cognitive defusion techniques to create distance from anxious thoughts.

PANIC DISORDER SPECIFIC INTERVENTIONS
Interoceptive exposure: Deliberately inducing feared physical sensations to reduce fear of sensations.
Breathing retraining: Diaphragmatic breathing to address hyperventilation patterns.
Safety behavior elimination: Gradually removing crutches and safety behaviors that maintain panic.
Psychoeducation about the panic cycle and the role of catastrophic misinterpretation.

SOCIAL ANXIETY SPECIFIC INTERVENTIONS
Social skills training when actual deficits in social skills are present.
Video feedback to correct distorted negative self-perception during social situations.
Attention training away from self-focused attention toward the external environment.
Behavioral experiments to test predictions about negative evaluation by others.

PHARMACOTHERAPY FOR ANXIETY
First-line medications: SSRIs and SNRIs require 4 to 6 week trial before assessing full response
Adjunctive options: Buspirone specifically for generalized anxiety disorder
Short-term acute management: Low-dose benzodiazepines for brief periods only
Performance anxiety: Beta-blockers for situational use only not for ongoing treatment
All medication decisions require consultation with prescribing physician
