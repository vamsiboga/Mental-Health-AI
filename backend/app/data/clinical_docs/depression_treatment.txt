EVIDENCE-BASED DEPRESSION TREATMENT GUIDELINES

FIRST-LINE PSYCHOTHERAPY TREATMENTS

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT):
CBT is the gold standard psychotherapy for major depressive disorder.
Typically delivered in 12 to 20 weekly sessions with a trained therapist.
Core focus: identifying negative automatic thoughts, behavioral activation, and cognitive restructuring.
The cognitive triangle connects thoughts, feelings, and behaviors in a cycle.
Evidence shows CBT is equivalent to medication for mild to moderate depression.
CBT skills can be practiced independently to prevent relapse after treatment ends.

Interpersonal Therapy (IPT):
IPT focuses on interpersonal role disputes, grief, role transitions, and interpersonal deficits.
Delivered in 12 to 16 weekly sessions with a trained IPT therapist.
Particularly effective for depression related to relationship difficulties or major life changes.
Addresses how interpersonal problems contribute to and maintain depressive symptoms.

Behavioral Activation:
Targets avoidance and withdrawal behaviors that are common in depression.
Uses structured scheduling to increase engagement with rewarding and meaningful activities.
Activity monitoring and scheduling are core components of behavioral activation.
Can be effectively delivered by non-specialist clinicians with proper training.

PHARMACOTHERAPY GUIDELINES
First-line medications: SSRIs including fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram, paroxetine, citalopram
Onset of action: Initial response typically seen at 2 to 4 weeks after starting medication
Full therapeutic benefit: Usually achieved at 4 to 8 weeks of consistent treatment
Minimum treatment duration: 6 to 12 months for a first depressive episode
Always consult with prescribing physician for all medication decisions and adjustments

COMBINED TREATMENT
CBT combined with antidepressant medication is superior to either treatment alone for moderate to severe depression.
Combined treatment significantly reduces relapse rates compared to medication alone.
Strongly recommended for recurrent depression or chronic depressive presentations.

LIFESTYLE INTERVENTIONS WITH EVIDENCE
Exercise: 30 to 45 minutes of aerobic exercise 3 to 5 times per week shown to reduce PHQ-9 scores by 2 to 4 points
Sleep hygiene: Consistent wake times every day, limit caffeine after noon, avoid screens before bed
Nutrition: Mediterranean diet associated with significantly lower depression risk in research studies
Social support: Encourage active maintenance of social connections even when patient prefers isolation

TREATMENT MONITORING SCHEDULE
Administer PHQ-9 at every clinical appointment to objectively track treatment response
Assess medication side effects at 1 to 2 weeks after any medication initiation or change
Evaluate treatment response formally at 4 to 6 weeks and escalate if less than 50% improvement
Consider referral to psychiatrist if two or more adequate medication trials have failed

RELAPSE PREVENTION
Continue antidepressant medications for 6 to 12 months after achieving full remission
For recurrent depression with 3 or more episodes consider indefinite maintenance treatment
Continuation of CBT skills practice independently reduces relapse risk by approximately 50%
Schedule regular booster sessions every 3 months after completing acute phase treatment
