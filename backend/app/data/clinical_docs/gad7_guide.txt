GAD-7 GENERALIZED ANXIETY DISORDER SCALE - CLINICAL GUIDE

OVERVIEW
The GAD-7 is a validated 7-item anxiety screening tool used in clinical practice.
Each item is scored 0 (Not at all) to 3 (Nearly every day). Maximum score is 21.
Validated for generalized anxiety disorder and also detects panic disorder and social anxiety.

THE 7 QUESTIONS
1. Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge
2. Not being able to stop or control worrying
3. Worrying too much about different things
4. Trouble relaxing
5. Being so restless that it is hard to sit still
6. Becoming easily annoyed or irritable
7. Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen

SCORING INTERPRETATION
Score 0-4: Minimal anxiety. No clinical action required unless patient reports significant distress.
Score 5-9: Mild anxiety. Monitor symptoms. Psychoeducation and lifestyle modifications recommended.
Score 10-14: Moderate anxiety. Consider CBT referral. Evaluate need for medication consultation.
Score 15-21: Severe anxiety. Active treatment indicated. Referral to mental health specialist recommended immediately.

CLINICAL CONSIDERATIONS
- GAD-7 screens for generalized anxiety but may also detect panic disorder, social anxiety, and PTSD
- High scores on items 1 and 2 suggest generalized anxiety pattern
- High scores on item 5 may indicate panic disorder when very elevated
- Comorbid depression is extremely common - always administer PHQ-9 alongside GAD-7
- Consider trauma history screening if scores are persistently high

TREATMENT GUIDELINES BY SEVERITY
Mild (5-9): Psychoeducation about anxiety, relaxation techniques, sleep hygiene, regular exercise
Moderate (10-14): CBT referral, consider SSRI or SNRI evaluation, structured problem-solving therapy
Severe (15-21): Immediate mental health referral, pharmacotherapy evaluation, consider intensive outpatient

CBT APPROACHES FOR ANXIETY
Cognitive Restructuring: Identifying and challenging anxious thoughts and catastrophic thinking
Exposure Therapy: Graduated systematic exposure to feared situations using SUDS scale 0-100
Relaxation Training: Progressive muscle relaxation and diaphragmatic breathing techniques
Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy: Integration of mindfulness with cognitive restructuring

MEDICATION CONSIDERATIONS
First-line medications: SSRIs such as sertraline and escitalopram, SNRIs such as venlafaxine
Second-line options: Buspirone for generalized anxiety, TCAs for refractory cases
Acute management: Low-dose benzodiazepines for short-term only due to dependence risk
Note: Always consult prescribing physician for all medication decisions

TREATMENT RESPONSE MONITORING
Treatment response: 50% or greater reduction in GAD-7 score from baseline
Full remission: GAD-7 score below 5
Repeat GAD-7 at every clinical appointment to track progress
