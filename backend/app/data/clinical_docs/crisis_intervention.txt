MENTAL HEALTH CRISIS INTERVENTION PROTOCOLS

DEFINITION OF MENTAL HEALTH CRISIS
A mental health crisis is any situation in which a person behavior puts them at risk of hurting
themselves or others, or prevents them from being able to care for themselves or function safely.

COLUMBIA SUICIDE SEVERITY RATING SCALE - C-SSRS
The C-SSRS assesses five key components:
1. IDEATION: Is the patient thinking about suicide? Passive wish to be dead vs active suicidal ideation
2. PLAN: Does the patient have a specific plan for how they would do it?
3. MEANS: Does the patient have access to the means such as firearms or medications?
4. INTENT: Does the patient intend to act on their plan in the near future?
5. BEHAVIOR: Has the patient engaged in any preparatory behavior or made past attempts?

RISK STRATIFICATION
Low Risk: Passive ideation only, no specific plan, no intent to act, no means access, strong protective factors present
Moderate Risk: Active suicidal ideation present, vague or general plan, some intent, protective factors still present
High Risk: Active ideation with specific plan, access to means, low protective factors, history of past attempts
Imminent Risk: Stated intent to act immediately, has means readily available, no protective factors, requires emergency intervention

IMMEDIATE CLINICAL ACTIONS BY RISK LEVEL
Low Risk: Create safety plan, provide crisis hotline numbers, increase appointment frequency, remove means if possible
Moderate Risk: Detailed safety plan, contact emergency support person, daily check-in calls, consider voluntary hospitalization
High Risk: Strongly recommend voluntary hospitalization, contact emergency services if patient refuses, do not leave alone
Imminent Risk: Call 911 immediately, do not leave patient alone under any circumstances, activate emergency protocol

SAFETY PLANNING - SIX COMPONENTS
1. Warning signs that a crisis may be developing for this patient
2. Internal coping strategies the patient can use alone
3. Social contacts who can provide distraction and support
4. People the patient can ask for help when crisis escalates
5. Professionals and agencies to contact in a crisis situation
6. Steps to make the home environment safe by removing means

CRISIS RESOURCES - PROVIDE THESE TO EVERY AT-RISK PATIENT
988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: Call or text 988 - available 24 hours 7 days a week
Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741 - free confidential text support
SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 - free confidential 24 hour service
International Association for Suicide Prevention: https://www.iasp.info/resources/Crisis_Centres/
Emergency Services: Call 911 if there is immediate danger to life

DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
Document all risk assessments with clinical rationale for risk level determination
Document safety plan creation and patient verbal agreement to the plan
Document notifications made to emergency contacts when clinically appropriate
Document all crisis interventions with timestamps and actions taken
Document follow-up plan and next appointment scheduled

DE-ESCALATION TECHNIQUES
Speak calmly and at a slow measured pace to model regulation
Validate the patient feelings without reinforcing crisis behavior patterns
Remove or reduce immediate environmental stressors when possible
Involve trusted support persons with patient explicit consent
Offer immediate concrete options rather than overwhelming long-term plans
Use open-ended questions to understand the patient experience
